In Alberta, inspection reports are not just good practice. They are part of the rules for residential tenancies, and they matter when it is time to return a security deposit. Here is a plain-language overview for landlords.

## The basics

- **Both inspections are required.** The landlord and tenant inspect the home together at the start of the tenancy and again at the end.
- **Timing matters.** The move-in inspection is done within one week before or after the tenant takes possession, and the move-out inspection within one week before or after the tenant gives up possession.
- **It must be in writing.** Both parties sign the report, and the tenant gets a copy.
- **Deposits depend on it.** Without proper inspection reports, a landlord generally cannot deduct the cost of damage from the security deposit.

## What a good report includes

- Every room, listed separately, including closets, the garage and outdoor areas.
- Walls, floors, ceilings, windows, doors and fixtures.
- Appliances, with notes on condition and cleanliness.
- Existing marks, wear or damage, described clearly. "Small scratch on kitchen floor near fridge" is far more useful than "floor OK".
- Dated photos, kept with the report.

## Tips for a smooth inspection

- **Book enough time.** A rushed walk-through leads to vague notes and disputes later.
- **Use the same form for move-in and move-out**, so conditions are easy to compare.
- **Remember normal wear and tear.** Faded paint or light wear from everyday living is expected over a tenancy and is not damage.
- **Keep it factual and calm.** If you and the tenant see something differently, note both views on the report.

## After move-out

Under Alberta rules, the landlord must return the deposit with any interest owing within 10 days after the tenant gives up possession, or provide a written statement of deductions within that time.
