Winter in the Rockies foothills arrives early and stays a while. Cold snaps, heavy snow and chinook swings are hard on homes. A few simple habits help prevent the most common winter problems, such as frozen pipes, ice build-up and high heating bills.

## For renters

- **Keep the heat on.** Even if you travel, leave the thermostat at a safe minimum (around 15°C is a common guideline) so pipes do not freeze.
- **Open cabinet doors** under sinks on outside walls during deep cold, so warm air reaches the pipes.
- **Do not block vents or baseboard heaters** with furniture or curtains.
- **Report drafts and problems early.** A sticking door, a cracked window seal or a furnace that cycles oddly is much easier to fix in November than in January.
- **Keep walkways clear** if your lease makes you responsible for snow removal, and use ice melt on steps.
- **Test your alarms.** Furnaces and fireplaces run hard in winter, so working smoke and carbon monoxide alarms are essential.

## For owners

- **Service the furnace before the cold sets in** and replace the filter. Ask about any carbon monoxide concerns.
- **Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses** and shut off exterior taps where there is an inside shut-off valve.
- **Check weatherstripping, caulking and attic insulation.** Small gaps add up to big heating costs.
- **Clean eavestroughs** so melt water drains away instead of forming ice dams and icicles.
- **Confirm who clears snow** and make sure it is clear in the lease.
- **Give tenants an emergency plan.** They should know where the water shut-off is and who to call if the heat goes out.

## If a pipe freezes

Turn off the water at the main shut-off if there is any sign of a leak, open the affected tap, and warm the area gently with a hair dryer or space heater. Never use an open flame. Then call your property manager right away.

A little attention in the fall saves a lot of trouble in February. If you rent with us and something does not seem right, report it through the tenant portal or call the office.
