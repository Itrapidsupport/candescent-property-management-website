Three years into running Candescent, I thought the hard part was behind me. Most new businesses don't make it past the first two years. I hadn't just survived that stretch, I was doing something the rest of the industry thought was impossible: running a property management brokerage with no real estate sales attached, and having people respond to it.

Then everything got harder at once.

## The Decision I Wasn't Ready to Make

Business wasn't running as normal. An experienced agent was leaving, a new agent was joining the team, and we'd taken on a contract for a neglected apartment building that needed serious attention. Right in the middle of all that, I lost my contract cleaner.

I had a choice to make. Find another cleaning company, or take the leap and hire an employee to bring that work in house.

If you've ever run a business or managed a property on your own, you already know this feeling. The moment where you have a choice: walk the same path you've walked before to solve the immediate problem, or choose a different one. It might be more uphill, but it's the one that helps make sure the problem doesn't show up again.

Looking back, that gap was telling me something. Replacing one contract cleaner yet again wasn't going to hold up as the business grew, and came with real risk of inconsistent quality and lack of availability. So instead of patching the old system, I had to ask myself if that was really solving the problem, or just delaying it until "next time."

## What I Was Actually Afraid Of

I wasn't sure I had enough work to support an employee. I wasn't sure how my clients would react. I wasn't prepared. I didn't even have a job description ready.

That's the part people don't always say out loud. Self-doubt in business rarely shows up as one big fear. It's a list of smaller ones, all landing at the same time.

What helped, eventually, was pulling those fears apart instead of carrying them as one heavy feeling. "What if this doesn't work" wasn't something I could plan around. "What if I don't have enough work to justify the cost" was. Once I broke it down that far, I could actually test it instead of just worrying about it.

## The Turning Point I Didn't See Coming

When the new apartment contract started, there were seven vacant units that needed maintenance and cleaning before they could go back on the market. I knew a cleaner who had just been let go from her summer job. The timing lined up almost too well.

I decided to try it and see how it worked out.

I didn't have certainty going in. What I had was enough evidence to justify the attempt, and that turned out to be enough. I've thought about that a lot since. Waiting for a guarantee before you act is often just another way of staying stuck.

## What Changed Once I Let Go

It hasn't been perfect. Managing an employee came with a steep learning curve, and we're still working through parts of it. But the role has grown from cleaning to also include maintenance coordination, and the impact has been real. Having someone dedicated to that work has allowed us to increase our response time. Quality has improved too.

The biggest shift wasn't in the day-to-day tasks though. It was in what I got back. Time. Space to work on the business instead of just being buried inside it.

## What This Means If You're Thinking About Hiring a Property Manager

I tell this story because it's not really about hiring a cleaner. It's about the moment you realize you can't do everything yourself, and the discomfort of handing something over anyway.

That's the same moment a lot of rental property owners hit. You've made a good investment. You did the research and ran the numbers. What you didn't fully account for was the ongoing weight of managing it, the late calls, the maintenance coordination, the tenant issues that always seem to land at the worst possible time.

You don't know, until you try it, how much time and mental space you could get back by handing that off to someone who does this work every single day. That's exactly what we do at Candescent.
